# Screenshots of real games — where each one came from

Every picture of somebody else's game that appears in a lesson is listed here, with the licence it
arrived under and the credit that has to appear beside it. If a lesson shows a game and it is not on
this list, that is a bug.

All files were fetched from Wikimedia (Commons or the English Wikipedia) on 21 Aug 2026, resized to
at most 780px wide — the widest a lesson column ever gets — and re-encoded so the whole set is
under 800 KB rather than 6 MB. School wifi is a design constraint.

**Two groups, and the difference matters.** Read the second one before showing this to anyone
outside the League.

---

**Pac-Man is the surprising one.** That screenshot is published under CC BY 3.0 with the
attribution going to Bandai Namco Entertainment America — so a genuine, recognisable, commercial
arcade classic is usable here with no fair-use argument needed at all. Same for Minecraft: those
four are official Xbox México captures released under CC BY 3.0.

**SuperTux and SuperTuxKart are open-source games**, which is why their screenshots are free. They
are also unusually useful for this course: SuperTux is a Mario-style platformer and SuperTuxKart is
a kart racer, so between them they cover two of the four genre directions a student can pick at a
checkpoint — and a student who wants to know how a level like that is actually built can go and read
the source.

## Group 2 — Non-free. Used as teaching commentary, on Jay's decision.

No free licence exists for these. They are hosted on Wikipedia under a fair-use rationale written
for Wikipedia's own articles, which is **not** a licence we inherit — our use is a separate claim.

That claim is a reasonable one: this is a course teaching about games, the images are low-resolution
as published, each is used to illustrate a specific point being made about game design, the source
is named, and nothing is being sold *as* the game. Teaching commentary is the strongest fair-use
footing there is. It is still a claim rather than a permission, and the honest answer if a publisher
ever objects is "we will take it down", not "here is our licence".

Jay decided to include these, having been told the above twice. Recorded here so the decision is
visible rather than buried.

### If the League wants Group 2 gone

Every one of these five has a free stand-in already in the repo, so removing them is an afternoon
rather than a rewrite:

- Asteroids → `endless-sky-title.jpg`, or the asteroids-style genre example the checkpoints ship.
- Space Invaders → the same; the point being made is about difficulty ramping, not about Taito.
- RuneScape → `supertux-worldmap.jpg` for progression, `runescape-hud` → any HUD, including our own.

### Games asked for and not included

- **Super Mario Bros.** — Nintendo publishes no reusable press assets and is the most protective
  rights holder in the industry. `supertux-level.jpg` is a freely-licensed platformer that makes
  every point a Mario screenshot would, and the lesson text can still *name* Mario, which needs no
  licence at all.
- **Fortnite** — Epic's fan content policy does not cover a paid course, and the Wikipedia article
  carries no usable screenshot.

Naming a game in prose is always fine. It is only the picture that needs a licence.
