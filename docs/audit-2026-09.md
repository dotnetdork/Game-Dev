# Ground-truth audit — September 2026

Dual lens: a 10–14-year-old on a 1366×768 school Chromebook, and an educational UX/systems critic.
Every number below was measured against the repository at commit `03fc6e1` — lesson files parsed
by script, the layout measured in a browser emulating 1366×768, the failure paths read in code.
Nothing here is inferred from the README.

## 2. Zone / checkpoint verdict

**Keep the board for the three design zones. Take it away from the three build zones.** Simplify the
one you keep until it stops being a Miro.

*Why keep it for design:* since the assistant started conducting the interview, the board has a
job a document cannot do — it is the visible transcript of a conversation, and a filled sticky
appearing with a flash is a genuinely good moment for a twelve-year-old. That is the metaphor that
fits: **not a whiteboard, a form that fills itself in while you talk**. Everything that makes it a
whiteboard fights that: infinite pan, zoom, a tools rail, "Ideas, unsorted", "Everything I want",
five blank placeholders. Concretely — fix the board to the viewport (fit-to-width, no pan, no
zoom control), lay regions out as a responsive grid instead of absolute `at:` boxes so
`cut-it-down` stops hiding under the chat at 1366, cut the rail to "+ note" and "read it back",
and drop the scratch regions from the two later design zones.

*Why remove it for build:* the three build zones are a kanban (Done / Now / Still ahead) whose
objectives are **code checks** — `config_changed`, `function_added`, `file_changed: game.js`
(`build-your-first-mechanic.md:48`). The student needs the editor and the objective side by side,
and the board is a modal that hides the editor. The `#zoneToCode` button (`index.html:377`) exists
precisely because the board is in the way. Render build objectives as a rail inside the Code view —
the goal-chip component already exists — with the zone-coach in the ordinary AI panel. That alone
fixes the cognitive switch for half the checkpoints.

*The receipt-vs-code gap is real and it is in the checks, not the prose.* `zone-coach.md` tells the
model to "tie it to their sheet", and the sheet is passed in. But the check that decides objective 2
is `function_added: true` — *any* function. A student can pass `build-your-first-mechanic` without
touching one word of their one-sheet, and the app will never know. The connection between the
sticky in module 1 and the code in module 4 is currently a suggestion to a language model. Make one
objective mechanically read the sheet (the "Built:" line already does the reverse — have it come
*before* wiring, and have the coach open by quoting the student's twist back to them).

*The repeat problem is in the files.* `your-one-sheet.md:25-28` asks for "The one thing you do",
"How you lose", "What makes it mine". `cut-it-down.md:28-30` asks for the same three again, six
lessons later, with a "now you know better" framing. To an adult that is revision. To a
twelve-year-old it is the same form twice. Fold `cut-it-down`'s one new slot ("My core loop") into
`the-core-loop`'s yourturn and retire the zone.

## 3. Course trim — from ~15–20 h to the 10–12 h window

**Where the time actually goes.** 25 content lessons × (≈7 min reading 900 words at a
12-year-old's technical-prose pace + 2 run cells + 2 quizzes + 1 lab at 8–15 min + 1 yourturn at
8–12 min) ≈ 30–40 min each = 12.5–16.5 h, plus 6 zones at 20–40 min = 2–4 h. **Realistic: 14.5–20 h.**
The "15 h" in the brief was optimistic. Hitting 10–12 needs ~35% out.

| Action | Lessons | Saves | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut** `from-project-to-playable` (L4) | 1 | ~40 min | 765 words on `.exe` vs web builds for a student who will never make an `.exe`. Its one useful sentence — "the web needs no install; a build is a link" — belongs in L1's engine list where Phaser is introduced. Its yourturn is *write a comment*. |
| **Merge** collision out of L2 into L19 | 2 → 1½ | ~15 min | Both recaps carry the same line verbatim ("overlap lets things pass through; collider pushes them apart"). L2 keeps position/velocity/gravity/feel; L19 owns overlap/collide where the Phaser API is. |
| **Merge** `where-ideas-come-from` + `scope-and-cutting` | 2 → 1 | ~30 min | "One verb done well" and "cut to one verb" are one lesson told twice. |
| **Retire** `cut-it-down` zone | 1 | ~30 min | See §2. Its new slot moves into `the-core-loop`'s yourturn (writes to `design.md`). |
| **Merge** `ship-it` + `launch-your-game` | 2 → 1 | ~30 min | The checklist *is* the launch zone's first objective. Three shipping lessons (L4, L30, L31) for one Publish button. |
| **One quiz per lesson**, not two | all | ~40 min | Fifty identical-shaped quizzes. Keep the one that tests the yourturn's idea. |
| **Cap prose at 600 words** | L1, L2, L10, L14, L30 | ~30 min | These are 900–1,090. The checker already enforces pacing; add a word ceiling as a report. |
| **Frontload identity** (no time cost) | L3 | 0 | Make L3's yourturn *buy* a character with the 250 starting Stars and swap the player sprite. The mechanics exist; only the content says "already own". Everyone's game stops looking identical at ~40 min instead of lesson 27. |

Total ≈ 3.5–4 h out → **11–16 h**. To guarantee the slow end of a real classroom lands inside 12,
make the lab *or* the yourturn required in modules 1–3, not both — the yourturn is the one that
touches their game, so keep that one mandatory.

Leave `sprites-and-animation`/`juice` where they are — the Store purchase at L3 buys the identity
those lessons were being asked to deliver, without moving them off the payoff.

## 4. Immediate fixes, ranked impact ÷ effort

1. **L3 yourturn → buy and swap the player** (`what-a-game-is-made-of.md:207`). One lesson file.
   Turns 250 idle Stars into the first moment every screen in the room looks different. Add
   `check: file_changed: player.js` so it is graded.
2. **Collapse the console by default** (`console-dock.js:40` → `false`). It already auto-opens on
   any game error. Editor and stage go from 413px to ~648px — **+57%** — on every Chromebook, for
   one character.
3. **Rewrite the first fifteen minutes of L1.** Cut to ≤500 words; replace the "world that will
   not tick" lab with a run cell whose only edit is a number; make the yourturn a change that
   *stays* (swap it with L2's gravity task — L1 changes `moveSpeed` and keeps it, L2 does gravity
   and feel). The lab is not wasted: it belongs in L17, where `function` and `if` are taught and
   it becomes a good exercise instead of a wall.

Cheap and worth doing the same afternoon: put a two-sentence "what a checkpoint is" line on the
first zone door and delete the tour claim from `course.yaml:6` or build the three-step tour it
promises; add a JSHint rule that refuses to Play a file containing `while (true)` or a `for` with
no increment, which does not solve the freeze but catches the commonest cause before it runs.

## 5. What is good, so it does not get cut by accident

The AI tone is right. `kid-communication.md` ("a friendly older sibling who codes"; never
"simply", "just", "obviously") and `tutor.md`'s escape hatch — "being stuck is not a teaching
opportunity, it is a dead end" — are better than most adult-facing tutors. The 922 openers in
`questions.yaml` read like a kid's actual questions ("Why does the game keep going when I am not
touching anything?"). The runtime-error wrapping and the syntax-error stage panel are exactly what
a beginner needs. The content checkers (`npm test`) are the most mature part of the project and are
the reason the vocabulary problem above is *known* rather than *suspected*. And the interview-fills-
the-board pattern is the first version of the checkpoint idea that a twelve-year-old would not
experience as homework.
