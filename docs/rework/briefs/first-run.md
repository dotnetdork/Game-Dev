# Brief: the first run — "Your first day at the studio"

**Status: v2, 2026-09-25 — awaiting Jay's confirmation.** Shaped with `/impeccable shape`. v1 was
"really close"; v2 folds in the studio reframe, research round 2 (`../02-research-round-2.md`) and
three decisions: launch departments are **Design, Engineering, Art (+ Animation/VFX), Audio**; the
"unfinished game" beat is **fix one thing, and the pick sets the path**; the mentor is **openly an
AI, and proud of it**.

## 1. Who arrives and why
A 10–13-year-old signing in for the first time, possibly new to computers. In the story: **their
first day as the new developer at a game studio.** Mode: Experience — a playful conversation, not a
form. This is the Front Desk's first quest, run by the mentor.

## 2. What success looks like
In about 5 minutes the kid has **played their own tiny game**, met the mentor, knows where their
game, the parts, the path and the mentor live, has met one department head, and the AI holds a
first **learner card**. Proof: their hands on their own game before five minutes.

## 3. Direction and structure

**The mentor** is the studio's AI — friendly, a bit of a show-off about the studio, and honest about
what it is. It can say "I'm a different kind of AI from the guard in your game," which turns the
research's biggest confusion (game AI vs generative AI) into a lesson.

**The engine builds itself around the kid.** Each beat unlocks one piece of the layout, and the
mentor names it in one sentence (feel → see → name):
mentor panel → **viewport** → **Play / Stop** → **parts list** (the engine's Hierarchy) → **the path**
→ the quiet stars bar.

**Discovery, not labels.** Open questions, then the mentor reflects back — "So, like Mario?" [Yes!]
[Not quite].

**The beats (~5 minutes):**
1. **Arrive.** A nearly empty screen: the mentor and one bubble. "Welcome to the studio! What should
   everyone call you?" — type a nickname, or pick from a few generated ones (slow typers).
2. **Two situation questions.** "You find a locked door. Do you… hunt for the key / smash it / sneak
   round the back / get a friend?" and "What's a game you could play all day? What's the best bit?"
   → reflect-back.
3. **The unfinished game.** The **viewport** appears with a tiny grey game: no colours, no sound, a
   boring rule, and a bug (you fall through the floor). "Our last intern left this… what should we
   fix first?" **[Colours] [Sound] [The rule] [The bug]** → Art / Audio / Design / Engineering. The
   kid watches their pick transform the game, and that department's head pops in to say hi. **The
   pick sets which department they visit first** and is the first real signal of what they love.
4. **Press Play.** **Play / Stop** appear. The mentor lets one change made while playing vanish on
   Stop — "every developer does that once!" — so the Play-mode trap is learned on day one, safely.
5. **Now YOUR game.** Dialogue options with reflect-backs: pick a hero · what you do (jump / dodge /
   collect / shoot) · a goal · a danger.
6. **Building.** The viewport assembles their game while the mentor narrates what it's using from
   their picks; the **parts list** fills in (Player, Coin, Enemy…) — "that's your Hierarchy — every
   real engine has one."
7. **Play it.** Their game, their keys. The peak.
8. **What's next.** **The path** appears with their first department; a glimpse of what's coming —
   "The art director can't wait to meet you…" The quiet stars bar appears last.

## 4. Scope and boundaries
One flow, 8 beats, grey-box at 1366×768. **Anti-goals:** forms; anything like a survey or a test;
more than one sentence to read at a time; asking for real name, age or school; drag or keyboard
shortcuts; a tour of panels.

## 5. States
Slow typer (generated names and options instead of typing) · "not quite" after a reflect-back (the
mentor asks another way) · picks nothing (the mentor suggests after a pause) · returning kid ("welcome
back", straight to their desk) · AI unavailable (the scripted spine still gets them to a playable
starter game — every beat has authored lines).

## 6. The learner card (seeded here, grows as they go)
- **Picks (closed tags):** kinds of fun (from the situations), favourite games, first-game
  ingredients, **first department** (from the fix pick), nickname.
- **Noticed:** reading, typing and clicking comfort; pace; how many choices suit them; curiosity.
- **Skills:** starts empty; the first-day concepts (viewport, Play mode, Hierarchy/parts) enter at
  "new".
- **Never:** real name, birthday, school, location, photos, voice, stored chat transcripts. The kid
  and teacher can see and reset the card.

## 7. Not to be invented by the wireframe
The mentor's name, look and voice · the department heads' names and looks · the exact situation
questions (content) · the unfinished game's art · what the generated nicknames are · whether a
teacher starts the class all at once.
